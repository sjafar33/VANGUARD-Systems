Moonbase Academy

Build a lunar base. Predict what happens. Test it. Change one thing. A mission trainer for ages 7 to 12, where every choice has a cost you can see.

The idea

A base builder that becomes
an engineering experiment.

Space games for children usually skip the trade-offs, or bury them in detail. Moonbase Academy asks one question a child can hold in their head, “Would your base keep working?”, and lets them find out the way an engineer does.

1

Build

Place solar arrays, batteries and shelter, then set an operating plan.

2

Predict

Say what will happen, and choose a reason why.

3

Test

Watch a deterministic simulation run the mission.

4

Explain

Compare the idea with the evidence, including when the idea was wrong.

5

Improve

Change one thing and test again, so the cause is clear.

One lesson, in real numbers

Saving electricity
spends something else.

A starter base has one solar array and one battery. In six hours of sunlight it stores 2.4 kWh. Six hours of shadow with the air maker on Full needs 3.6 kWh. The test stops at hour 10 of 12.

Build and plan

The base stays in view. The choices stay simple.

Equipment comes from a shop bar, like a building game. The operating plan opens as a panel above it: one part of the mission at a time, with the air maker on Off, Half or Full.

  • Each setting says what it does in a sentence a child can read.
  • Live notes show whether batteries are draining and whether the crew is using tank oxygen.
  • Example labs need no coins or unlocks, and never touch the player's saved game.

Predict with a reason

A guess is not enough. Say why.

Before every test the player picks an outcome and a reason. Some reasons are common misconceptions, such as “adding a battery makes more electricity.” The prediction never changes the physics: equipment and settings decide the result.

Evidence, not punishment

“Not yet” is a result you can use.

The result screen puts the player's idea beside what happened, checks the reason separately from the outcome, and suggests the next experiment. A correct forecast of failure is recognised as a good prediction, but it does not complete the mission.

  • “Batteries store energy; they cannot create it.”
  • Every number can be opened and inspected.
  • Try again returns to the plan, at the part worth changing.

Example labs

Ready to test in one tap.

A teacher or a judge can open the Shadow lab or the Storm lab straight from the Drills list. Add or remove arrays and batteries for free, keep the plan, and compare each test with the last one.

Four drills

Power, air, food, shelter, signal.

Each drill isolates one trade-off of running an outpost, and each has a harder version that unlocks after the first success.

Power and life support

Lights Out

Six hours of sunlight, six of shadow. Balance generation, storage and the air maker so the base never goes dark.

Food production

The Living Base

Grow lettuce through a 33-day crop period. Greenhouse light, research and the crew all draw on the same daily energy budget.

Radiation shelter

Storm Drill

Shelter the crew through an archived solar particle event from 10 September 2017, lasting 98 hours 5 minutes, and still finish a science task.

Communication delay

Signal Lag

Stop a rover from Earth. A radio signal takes about 1.28 seconds each way, so the rover keeps moving after you decide.

Plus Test My Base, which runs the shadow and storm tests on the base the player actually built, and short lessons at three question levels.

What is a game rule

Equipment sizes (1 kW per solar array in sunlight, 4 kWh per battery), the simple air maker, and the six-hour shadow are training rules written for this game. A real lunar night is far longer.

What we do not claim

  • No live data feeds: historical records are embedded and labelled archived.
  • No radiation dose is calculated.
  • Water, CO₂, temperature and full nutrition are outside this prototype.
  • No study of learning effectiveness with children has been run.

How we built it

Native, offline, and honest.

A native iPhone and iPad app for iOS 17 and later. No accounts, no ads, no analytics, no network.

Technology

  • Swift, SwiftUI and SpriteKit, with Core Graphics for all artwork.
  • A deterministic resource engine, independent of animation timing.
  • Art and audio are drawn and synthesised by project code.
  • XCTest unit tests for the engine, and UI tests for the main flows.
  • Open source under the MIT license.

Made for different learners

  • Optional read-aloud for the guide's words.
  • Reduced motion, a calmer Focus view, and step-by-step pacing.
  • Layouts for very large text sizes.
  • Three question levels in the lessons.

Use of AI

AI coding tools (Claude Code and Codex) assisted with implementation, tests, debugging, procedural artwork code and documentation under Sam Jafari's direction. The app itself has no AI feature at runtime. The full disclosure is in AI_USE.md.
